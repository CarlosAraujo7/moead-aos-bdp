## Cover letter (draft)

Dear Editor,

We submit "[Title]" for consideration as a Theory and Methodology paper (Discrete Optimization / Computational Intelligence). The paper studies the bi-objective versions (MaxSum and MaxMin) of three dispersion problems — the maximum diversity problem and its capacitated and generalised variants — on 443 benchmark instances. A MOEA/D with a pool of eight neighbourhood operators is compared with NSGA-II, GRASP, variable neighbourhood search and tabu search, with three replicates per instance, a frozen reference front and three quality indicators and, in a second stage, with baselines tuned by the same protocol as the proposed method and run for the same wall-clock time.

The main findings are: (i) scaling the objectives inside the Tchebycheff function is the most important design choice, with an effect that grows with instance size; (ii) MOEA/D stays ahead of tuned baselines on the constrained problems (clearly on the generalised problem; at equal time on the capacitated one) but is behind a tabu search, a VNS and a tuned GRASP on the unconstrained problem — a reversal that untuned baselines would hide; (iii) a controlled, replicated study of adaptive operator selection (several selectors including the published dynamic Thompson sampling and FRRMAB, rewards, hyper-parameters, budgets and contexts) shows that uniform selection from the pool is as good as any bandit on instances up to about 200 elements and on the capacitated problem, whereas HV-reward bandits win on very large or tightly constrained instances; (iv) a short single-objective MaxMin step brings the extreme of the front to within about 1% of the best published values on four of five capacitated-problem families and above the published values for the generalised problem; (v) the size of the selected set is a hidden dimension of the constrained bi-objective problems, which we quantify; (vi) the protocol, the data and the code are open, and our evaluator reproduces all 80 published solutions of the generalised-problem benchmark that we tested.

The bi-objective diversity problem for the unconstrained case was introduced by Colmenar, Martí and Duarte (2018), whose results we confirm and extend. To our knowledge, evolutionary multi-objective algorithms have not been evaluated before on the MaxSum + MaxMin versions of the capacitated and generalised dispersion problems, and operator-pool design and adaptive operator selection have not been studied in this setting `[repeat the systematic search before keeping this sentence]`. The work has not been published or submitted elsewhere. `[suggested reviewers, if the journal asks]`

Sincerely,
`[authors]`
